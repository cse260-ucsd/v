2D block tiling

2D block tiling gives each thread a 2 × 2 square of C. At every step it reads 2 numbers of As and 2 of Bs into registers and multiplies each a by each b, using every number twice. A block of 16 threads now covers 8 × 8 of C, halving its copies from global memory per number of C; the 16 × 16 matrices fit 4 such blocks.

In a real implementation

  1. Square and block sizes. Both are parameters to tune. A TM × TN square reads TM numbers of As and TN of Bs per step for TM · TN multiply-adds; a BM × BN block copies BM + BN numbers from global memory per step of k for BM · BN numbers of C. Bigger ones reuse more of what they read but need more registers and shared memory, so time a few combinations on the Tesla T4 at the matrix sizes in use.
  2. Registers. A thread needs TM · TN registers for its sums and TM + TN for a and b. A Tesla T4 SM (streaming multiprocessor) has 65,536 registers: 64 per thread when all 1,024 threads it can hold are there. Past that, fewer warps fit per SM, and each thread's TM · TN independent multiply-adds have to keep it busy instead.
  3. Banks. A warp's threads read Bs two numbers apart. Shared memory has 32 banks, successive 4-byte words in successive banks, so the 4 threads here hit 4 banks. A real warp of 32 along a row of the block puts two threads in each bank, and their reads take turns. CUTLASS lays out its shared memory reads to be conflict-free.
